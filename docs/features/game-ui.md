# Game UI design system

The game UI grows with every mechanic in the [design roadmap](../game/design-roadmap.md): terrain, units, buildings, trees, governors, seasons. The design system (backlog item G1) fixes the approaches once so each mechanic plugs into an existing slot instead of reshaping the screen. The rule file is `.claude/rules/game-ui.md`; the plan is [G1](../plans/done/G1-ui-design-system.md) (done).

**How it stays expandable:** every extension point is a registry or a slot, and a test fails when a new game thing lacks its UI part. A ruleset unit or terrain without an icon fails `GameSpriteTests`; a terrain without colours fails `GameUiTests`.

## Game screen layout (full-screen view)

A started game (`Features/Games/Components/GameTable`) is the full-screen game view (F16, [plan](../plans/done/F16-full-screen-game-view.md)). The page uses `Layout/GameLayout`: the app header (`Layout/AppHeader`, shared with `MainLayout`) with a "Back to my games" button instead of the sidebar, and a body without padding. The board fills everything below the header, and the controls float over it as cards in overlay slots (`wwwroot/css/game-ui.css`). The slots (`.hud`) take no clicks themselves, so a drag between two cards still pans the board; only their cards do.

| Slot | Component | Holds | Grows with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Board (`.hud-board`) | `Board/GameBoard` | The board ([game board](#game-board)), pan and zoom, and its own controls: the lens toolbar on the left and the zoom and Fit buttons on the right, both just below the top strip. It opens on your units and cities (`BoardScene.Start`), or the whole map when you have none; Fit shows the whole map | map lenses, new scene layers |
| Top strip (`.hud-top`) | `Screen/TopBar` | The game's title and connection badge, your seat colour, "Turn n: your turn", the resources in all your cities (one `CostLabel` per ruleset resource, the sum of your cities' stock), on your turn "n units can still move" (`.next-unit`: own units with movement left and no job, from `GameUiService.IdleUnits`; a click selects the next one, wrapping round), Log (with the number of new entries), Full screen (where the browser has it), Resign (inline confirm) and End turn | season (G14): one element each, next to the resources |
| Notices (`.hud-notices`) | `Screen/NoticeStack` | Top centre, under the lens toolbar: the stale-command notice, the Api error, and the result of a finished game (`GameOverPanel`) | |
| Selection card (`.hud-selection`) | `Screen/SelectionCard` | Bottom left: the panel for the selection (`UnitPanel` with the unit's actions from `Screen/ActionBar` under it, or `CityPanel` with its Units, Buildings and Economy tabs), or a hint when nothing is selected. Its body scrolls inside the slot's height, and its button collapses it to its heading (`GameUiService.SelectionCollapsed`); the next selection opens it again | one panel per new selection kind (tile, building) |
| Attack forecast (`.board-anchor`) | `Screen/AttackForecast` in the board's `Anchored` slot | The combat forecast of a pending attack, beside the target tile ([interaction rules](#interaction-rules)) | |
| Log drawer (`.hud-log`) | `Screen/LogDrawer` | On the right, under the zoom buttons, while open: the game log ([game log](#game-log-in-the-log-drawer)) | |
| Large view | (added with its first user) | Large content that covers the board: tech and culture trees (G11), the city screen, governors (G12) | |

The top strip's height is kept in the screen's `--gv-hud-top` property by `wwwroot/js/game-screen.js` (a `ResizeObserver`, through `Services/GameScreenInteropService`), so the board's controls, the notices and the log drawer sit below it however much it wraps. The same module enters and leaves the browser's full screen (the Fullscreen API on the game screen element, which also hides the app header); Esc in the browser leaves it too, and `GameUiService.FullScreen` follows the browser's `fullscreenchange`. The button is hidden where the browser has no full screen for page elements (Safari on iPhone).

**Phones (narrower than 40rem):** the top strip keeps the connection badge, the turn, a More button and End turn; More opens the resources, "units can still move", Log, Full screen and Resign under them. The selection card and the attack forecast become bottom sheets across the full width, at most 45% of the screen high, and the forecast covers the card while it is open. The log drawer takes the top half.

## Interaction rules

| Rule | How |
|---|---|
| Select, then act | A click selects a unit or city; moves are clicks on highlighted tiles. An attack on a unit takes two steps: a click on the target marks it and shows the combat forecast beside the target, only the forecast's Attack button attacks (so a double-click or double-tap never attacks unseen), Cancel or Esc backs out. Capturing an undefended city is one click (nothing to forecast). Other actions are buttons in the selection card |
| Exact numbers on demand | `NumberTooltip`: hover shows the title; the info button shows it on click, Enter, Space or tap (no long press); Esc closes it |
| Destructive actions confirm inline | `ConfirmButton`: the first click asks, a second click on the confirm button acts, Cancel or Esc backs out (Resign today) |
| No accidental actions | A drag on the board is never a click; commands are sent one at a time; a link never acts on its own |
| Bridge mode | Build bridge (`GameUiService.ToggleBridge`) marks the neighbours the unit can bridge to (`BoardHighlights.Bridgeable`, from `GameEngine.BridgeTargets`, dashed `overlay bridgeable`); a click on one sends the bridge, any other click, the button again or Esc leaves the mode |
| Placing a building | Build <building> in the city panel's Buildings tab (`GameUiService.PlaceBuilding`; "Move <building>" for the one in production) sets `BoardSelection.Placing` and marks the tiles of the city's territory that can take it (`BoardHighlights.Placeable`, from `GameEngine.BuildingSites`, dashed `overlay placeable`), with a hint under the button, and the screen takes keyboard focus back (the panel's tabs keep Esc from it); a click on one sends `ProduceBuildingCommand` with that site, a click on its current site, any other click, the button again or Esc stops placing. The button is disabled with the reason of the tile that came closest (`GameEngine.CheckProduceBuilding`) when no tile can take it |
| Forecast beside the target | The board takes the forecast as its `Anchored` fragment with the target as `AnchorAt`, and keeps it next to the tile as the camera moves: it hands the element to `js/board3d/labels.js` (`Board3DInteropService.AnchorAsync`), which writes the tile's screen point as `--anchor-x`, `--anchor-y` and `--anchor-flip` only when the camera moves. The CSS puts the card on the side of the tile with more room (right, or left past 55% of the width) and keeps it inside the board |
| Keyboard | Every action is a button (Tab reaches it); Esc closes the log drawer, else cancels a pending attack, bridge mode or placing a building, else clears the selection (`GameUiService.Escape`). The screen takes keyboard focus on arrival and after each command (whose disabled buttons would drop it); an Esc handled by an open `ConfirmButton` or `NumberTooltip` stops there |

## UI state (GameUiService)

`Features/Games/Services/GameUiService` holds the UI state of one game screen: the selection (`BoardSelection`) and its highlights (`BoardHighlights`, from the Core previews). `GameTable` creates one per game and cascades it (`CascadingValue IsFixed`), so `SelectionCard`, `ActionBar` and the other cards read the selection instead of receiving it through parameter chains. It holds no game state: the view stays the server's. A new view calls `Refresh`, which drops a selection that is no longer visible, a pending attack target (`BoardSelection.Target`) the unit can no longer fight, bridge mode (`BoardSelection.Bridge`) with nothing left to bridge, and placing a building (`BoardSelection.Placing`) with no tile left to take it. It also holds the active map lens ([map lenses](#map-lenses)), the screen's own state (the collapsed selection card `SelectionCollapsed`, the log drawer `LogOpen` and the newest entry seen in it `LogSeen` with `UnreadLog`, browser full screen `FullScreen`); later UI state (an open large view) is added here.

## Design tokens, themes and colours

All colours are CSS custom properties in `wwwroot/css/tokens.css` (`--gv-<group>-<name>`); `GameUiTests.Css_UsesColourValuesOnlyInTokens` rejects colour values in any other CSS file, and `Razor_WritesNoColourValues` rejects them in component attributes.

| Tokens | Use |
|---|---|
| `--gv-seat-1` … `--gv-seat-4` | One colour per seat in turn order (`PlayerView.Players`); classes `seat-1`… set `fill` and `color` (`Helpers/Seats`). More than four seats (F10) needs more tokens |
| `--gv-terrain-<id>` | One colour per ruleset terrain. The board reads them directly (`palette.js`) and fades remembered tiles towards `--gv-board3d-memory`; `board.css` maps each id with one `.terrain-<id>` rule for the terrain icons |
| `--gv-board3d-*`, `--gv-overlay-*`, `--gv-lens-*`, `--gv-selected`, `--gv-label*`, `--gv-icon-mark`, `--gv-health-*`, `--gv-river*`, `--gv-bridge*` | The board (`--gv-overlay-target` marks a pending attack target, `--gv-health-*` the health bar in a hurt unit's banner) |
| `--gv-text`, `--gv-surface*`, `--gv-border`, `--gv-accent`, `--gv-danger` | Panels and components, built on Radzen's `--rz-*` theme variables |
| `--gv-space-1..3`, `--gv-radius`, `--gv-icon-size` | Spacing and shape |

**Light and dark:** the app follows the system setting. `index.html` loads Radzen's `material.css` for `prefers-color-scheme: light` and `material-dark.css` for dark, and `tokens.css` overrides the board tokens under the same media query. There is no switch in the UI yet.

**CSS files** (global, no scoped `.razor.css`): `tokens.css` (values), `app.css` (Blazor boot screen and error UI), `board.css` (the board and its labels, seats, the terrain icon mapping and the gallery's river swatches), `game-ui.css` (the full-screen view and its cards, components and the design gallery).

## Icon set (GameSprite)

`Components/Game/GameSprite.razor` holds every game icon as a `<symbol>`; the game screen renders it once (hidden) and every `<svg>` on the page draws an icon with `<use href="#id">`, so the board's banners and the panels share one set, all self-hosted and CSP-safe (no external files, no raw markup).

| Symbol id | For |
|---|---|
| `unit-<id>` | Each ruleset unit (`Icons.Unit`) |
| `terrain-<id>` | Each ruleset terrain (`Icons.Terrain`): a hex swatch filled with the terrain colour plus a `.terrain-mark` drawing (trees, humps, peaks, waves; none for plains), so terrain is never told by colour alone in the panels; on the board each terrain has its own shape (`Helpers/TerrainReliefs`) |
| `city` | Cities |
| `improvement-<id>`, `building-<id>`, `resource-<id>` | Each ruleset improvement (outlined lines), building and resource (`Icons.Improvement`, `Icons.Building`, `Icons.Resource`) |
| `ui-movement`, `ui-sight`, `ui-production`, `ui-health`, `ui-strength`, `ui-turns`, `ui-bridge` | UI glyphs (`Icons` constants) |

Shapes sit in a 26×26 box centred on 0,0. The main shape takes its fill from the `<use>` (a seat or terrain class); `.icon-mark` draws the white mark inside unit symbols and `.icon-line` draws outlined glyphs in the text colour. `GameIcon` draws one icon at text size; it is decorative unless `Label` is set (then `role="img"` with that name). `GameSpriteTests` checks every ruleset unit, terrain, improvement, building and resource and every `Icons` constant has a symbol.

## Game log in the log drawer

`Features/Games/Components/GameLog` lists the player's log (`GetGameResponse.Log`, [games](games.md#game-log)) in the log drawer (`Screen/LogDrawer`): one "Turn n" group per round, newest first, and in each round the newest line on top. `Helpers/GameLogFormatter` turns an event into one sentence with ruleset names and seats ("Your Archer hit Seat 2's Soldier: 22 damage, 6 taken", "An unseen Seat 2 Archer hit your Soldier"); `.yours` lines (your own actions) carry the accent border. A line with a tile has a Show button: it centres the board on the tile (`GameUiService.Focus`, `GameBoard.Focus`) and selects your unit or city there. The drawer is closed when the screen opens; the top strip's Log button opens and closes it (`GameUiService.ToggleLog`, `aria-pressed`) and counts the entries that arrived since it was last open (`.log-unread`; what happened before the screen opened is not new). Its close button or Esc closes it.

## Board animations

`Features/Games/Services/BoardAnimationService` (F11b, one per `GameTable`) plays the new log entries of each newer view on the board, in order. It is the one source of what plays and when: the board receives its `BoardMotion` and draws it through `SceneMotionBuilder` (see [game board](#game-board)). The view itself is applied at once, so panels and buttons never wait; only `BoardMotion` (unit positions, ghosts, effects) changes while it plays.

| Event | On the board |
|---|---|
| `unit_moved` | The unit walks tile by tile (`Step`, 120 ms a tile); a unit no longer in the view walks as a ghost |
| `unit_attacked` | The attacker lunges a third of the way and back (`Lunge`, 160 ms), then both damage numbers float up (`.board3d-damage`) |
| `unit_destroyed` | A ghost of the unit fades and sinks |
| `city_founded`, `city_captured`, `improvement_built`, `bridge_built` | The tile pulses (a growing ring) |

- Each entry plays once (by `sequence`); the log the game opens with plays nothing.
- A newer view or a tile click finishes everything at once, so input is never blocked.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` (read once by `GameTable` through `GameScreenInteropService.PrefersReducedMotionAsync`, and in CSS): units don't walk or lunge, and effects only fade.
- `BoardMotion` places units and effects in `HexLayout` points; `SceneMotionBuilder` turns them into board units, so the service stays free of the renderer.

## Game board

`Components/Board/GameBoard` is a WebGL board in a low-poly diorama style, built in F15 ([plan](../plans/done/F15-3d-board.md)) and the only board since F17 retired the SVG board ([plan](../plans/done/F17-retire-svg-board.md)). It draws the terrain, rivers and bridges, improvements and buildings, territory borders, units and cities, the selection, highlights and both map lenses, and it plays the animations ([board animations](#board-animations)). Without WebGL the board says it can't show the map; the cards over it still work.

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| `Helpers/BoardSceneBuilder` | Turns the `PlayerView` into a `Models/BoardScene`: one `SceneTile` per tile (axial `q`/`r`, centre from `HexLayout` scaled so a corner is 1 unit away, terrain id, visibility, height and shape), the map's rectangle and the rectangle to open on (the player's own pieces, else the map). What shows is decided here, in C# |
| `Helpers/SceneLayerBuilder` | The layers above the tiles: `SceneRiver` per known river edge (visible and explored tiles; the water at the lower of both tiles' known surfaces, a lake's being its water, so the higher tile's side is the bank), `SceneBridge` per bridge in sight (a deck from 0.45 inside one tile to 0.45 inside the other, sloping between their surfaces), `SceneFeature` per improvement or building in sight and per building the player's cities are producing (planned, drawn see-through), in the tile's upper-left corner so the middle stays free for a piece, and `SceneBorder` per territory edge in sight (inset 0.12 into the owned tile, in the owner's seat) |
| `Helpers/RiverReliefs`, `Helpers/FeatureModels` | The look per ruleset river kind (width and deep colour: stream 0.09, river 0.17, deep river 0.27) and the `Enums/FeatureModel` per improvement and building (farm field, lumber camp logs and hut, mine rock and entrance, army camp tents, campus dome, trade post stall, culture centre hall, harbour pier). Unknown ids fall back to a river, a field or a hall; `RiverReliefsTests` and `FeatureModelsTests` fail when a ruleset id has no entry, and `GameUiTests.Board3DModels_HaveABuilderPerFeatureModel` when a model kind has no builder in `models.js`. The JS builds by model kind, never by ruleset id |
| `Helpers/TerrainReliefs` | The look per ruleset terrain: tile height and `Enums/TileShape` (plains flat, desert dunes, forest trees, hills humps, mountains snow-capped peaks, lake water). Presentation only, so not in the ruleset; `TerrainReliefsTests` fails when a ruleset terrain has no entry. Tiles are low (a hint of height, hills and mountains a little higher) and nearly touch, with a thin seam. Unexplored tiles lie flat under a mist |
| `Services/Board3DInteropService` | Mounts the canvas (`false` without WebGL: the board then says so), sends scenes, overlays, motions, Fit, focus and zoom, and relays clicks back as `HexCoord` (`OnTileClicked`) |
| `Helpers/SceneMotionBuilder` | Converts `BoardAnimationService`'s `BoardMotion` (`HexLayout` points) into a `Models/SceneMotion` in board units: `SceneMotionPosition` per moving unit (by its id, `ScenePiece.Unit`) with the surface of the tile under the point (`HexLayout.Nearest`), `SceneGhost` per ghost (`ScenePieceBuilder.Ghost`: model, seat and icon), `SceneEffect` per damage number or pulse, and the service's step, lunge and effect durations, so the JS never keeps its own copy |
| `wwwroot/js/board3d/` | `board.js` (entry), `scene.js` (renderer, sky and sun light with shadows; builds again only the layers whose part of the scene changed; draws on demand and every frame only while something animates), `gestures.js` (the mouse or one finger drags to pan, two fingers pinch to zoom around them and pan with them, the wheel zooms at the pointer, a press that hardly moved is a click), `camera.js` (north-up camera that tilts as it zooms; panning grabs the ground, zooming keeps the ground under the pointer or the fingers in place), `bench.js` (the numbers for the bench page, below), `terrain.js` (one flat-shaded hex prism per tile, decorations as instanced low-poly models, kept clear of a tile's improvement or building), `rivers.js` (water strips with round joints, plank bridges with rails), `models.js` (one small model per `FeatureModel` from boxes, pyramids, cylinders and a dome; a building's roof or flag in its seat colour), `pieces.js` (one model per `PieceModel`: soldier, spearman, archer, builder and settler are baked characters on a base in seat colour, the horseman a baked rider on a horse built in code, the city built in code; an enemy's darker), `overlays.js` (border dashes in seat colour, tinted tile tops and the selection ring), `labels.js` (HTML over the canvas: banners, city names, lens labels, edge badges, damage numbers), `motion.js` (the animations, below), `shapes.js` (instancing and freeing), `palette.js` (colours; `tint` reads an `rgba()` token into a colour and an opacity) |
| `Helpers/ScenePieceBuilder`, `Helpers/PieceModels` | The scene's `ScenePiece`s: cities first, then units, each with its tile, height, `Enums/PieceModel`, seat, own flag, health share (of the ruleset's full health) and sprite icon, a city also with its name; a unit in a city stands off its centre so both show. `PieceModels` maps each ruleset unit to its model (a new unit falls back to the soldier; `PieceModelsTests` fails when a ruleset unit has no entry) |
| `Helpers/BoardOverlayBuilder` | The `Models/BoardOverlay` from the selection, highlights and active lens: `OverlayTile`s tinted with a token name (the lens's marks with their labels, then reachable, attackable, target, bridgeable and placeable tiles drawn over them), `OverlayEdge`s for the lens's edge badges, and the selected unit's or city's position for the ring |

- **Clicks and touch:** a press that moves more than 4 pixels (10 for a finger, since a tap wobbles) is a drag; two fingers pinch to zoom around their midpoint and pan with it, lifting one hands the pan to the other without a jump, and a gesture that ever had two fingers never ends in a click; a click picks the tile under the pointer by raycasting the tiles and the pieces (a tall figure covers the tile behind it, so a hit on a piece picks the piece's tile) and goes through `TileClicked` to `GameTable`, which selects, moves, attacks, bridges and places (`BoardClickResolver`).
- **Unit models:** units are characters from the CC0 KayKit Adventurers pack (knight for the soldier and, with a spear, the spearman; rogue with a crossbow for the archer; barbarian with an axe for the builder; mage for the settler; a seated knight for the horseman's rider). `tools/unit-models` bakes them once into `wwwroot/models/units/<name>.json`: posed, the texture read into vertex colours, no skeleton or animation, and split into the character's own colours and its seat parts (cape, shield or hat), which the board multiplies by the seat colour. `pieces.js` fetches them once when the board mounts (about 280 KB gzipped) and draws the units' bases until they arrive. `--gv-board3d-figure` is the light on the characters, dimmer in the dark theme. Their own colours are the only colours on the board that are not tokens: they come from the KayKit artwork, and the theme reaches them only through that light and the seat colour.
- **Pieces, banners and labels:** every unit and city carries a small HTML banner with its `GameSprite` symbol (an SVG `use`) and a health bar (low at a third or less), and a city its name, so a piece reads at any zoom. The labels live in one `.board3d-labels` layer over the canvas, re-placed when the camera moves (otherwise only new labels and those following a moving piece; the page is written to only where a label's place or visibility changed), with `pointer-events: none` and `aria-hidden` (the selection card says the same). They are built with `createElement`, `createElementNS` and `textContent` only, never HTML strings.
- **Overlay:** the selection, highlights and lens go to the renderer as a `BoardOverlay` apart from the scene (`Board3DInteropService.RenderOverlayAsync`), so selecting a unit or switching a lens rebuilds only the tinted tile tops, the ring and the labels, never the terrain or the pieces. `GameBoard` sends it only when the drawn view, selection, highlights or lens changes, and hosts the `LensToolbar` next to the zoom buttons.
- **Animations:** `GameBoard` sends the `SceneMotion` (`Board3DInteropService.RenderMotionAsync`) whenever `BoardMotion` changes, after the scene of the same view. `motion.js` draws it: a unit the motion moves is taken out of the shared instanced meshes and built as an actor of its own (`pieces.js` `buildActor`), so it can move and turn alone; the shared meshes are rebuilt only when a unit starts animating and once everything has settled, never per step. Each new place starts a glide from where the unit is drawn: a move of a tile or more is a step (the service's 120 ms, a small hop, the ground's height in between), a shorter one a lunge (half the lunge time each way); the unit turns to face the way it goes and back to the camera on its tile, and its banner follows it. Because the view is applied at once, a walk's first place (a step or more from the unit's tile in the new scene) is drawn at once rather than glided to. Ghosts are actors outside the scene: a walking one glides, a fading one fades and sinks over the effect time with its own see-through materials and no shadow. Damage numbers are HTML labels that float up and fade (`.board3d-damage`, `gv-float`); a pulse is a flat hexagonal ring in `--gv-selected` that grows and fades on the tile, drawn over the pieces. An empty motion (a click, a newer view, or the end) puts every unit back on its tile at once and drops ghosts and effects; an effect or fading ghost that has played out never plays again while the service still lists it. Reduced motion needs nothing of its own: the service already skips walks and lunges, damage labels only fade (`prefers-reduced-motion` in `board.css`), pulses don't grow and ghosts don't sink. The selection ring stays on the selected unit's tile while it walks.
- **Lifetime:** leaving the board (another page) unmounts the renderer and frees its WebGL context, its shared shapes and materials included (Three.js keeps the renderer referenced from them otherwise).
- **Colours:** every colour comes from `tokens.css`: the `--gv-terrain-<id>` palette for tile tops and models, explored tiles faded towards `--gv-board3d-memory`, the `--gv-board3d-*` tokens for sky, sun, backdrop, snow, trunks, lake beds, the mist, and model walls, roofs, fields, rock and tent canvas, the `--gv-river*` tokens for rivers (explored ones in their explored shade), `--gv-bridge` for timber and `--gv-seat-<n>` for borders and building roofs. Light and dark switch with the system setting (the board rebuilds every layer on change, keeping the camera and any animation). `GameUiTests.Board3DScripts_ReadOnlyTokensThatExist` fails when a script names a token `tokens.css` doesn't define (it would draw grey), and `Board3DTokens_HaveADarkValue` when a `--gv-board3d-*` token has no dark value. `GameUiTests.Board3DScripts_TakeColoursOnlyFromTokens` rejects colour literals in `js/board3d`.
- **Fog:** unexplored tiles lie flat under one seamless sheet of mist (`--gv-board3d-mist`), clear at every corner that touches a known tile, so the fog thins out softly at the edge of what the player has seen instead of ending in a hard line.
- **Updates:** the scene is sent only for a new view version (the live update echoing the player's own command doesn't rebuild it); selection, panning and zooming never rebuild it. A game log focus from before the board was opened doesn't move it; it opens on the player's pieces.

## Map lenses

A lens marks every known tile with one kind of information, instead of cluttering the base map.
- **A lens** is a sealed `Features/Games/Lenses/XxxLens` implementing `IMapLens` (id, name, toolbar icon, and `MarkFor(TileView)` → a `Models/LensMark` with a label, a tint class and a sentence), registered in `DependencyInjection` as `IMapLens`. `NamingConventionTests.ClientMapLenses_AreSealedLensesImplementingIMapLens` and `FolderStructureTests` (`*Lens.cs` only) keep the folder to that.
- **State:** `GameUiService.ActiveLens`, changed by `ToggleLens` (one on at a time; toggling the active one turns it off). Escape clears the selection, not the lens.
- **Toolbar:** `Components/Board/LensToolbar` in the board's top-left corner (below the top strip in the game screen), one toggle button per registered lens (`aria-pressed`, keyboard reachable). It reads the cascaded `GameUiService`, so it renders nothing outside a game screen.
- **Edges:** a lens may also mark the edges a tile stores (`IMapLens.EdgeMarksFor(TileView)` → `Models/EdgeMark`, none by default), drawn as a small badge at the edge's middle (`.board3d-edge-badge.<class>`).
- **Drawing:** `Helpers/BoardOverlayBuilder` puts the active lens in the board's overlay: per marked tile a tint (the `--gv-lens-<class>` token) and its label, then the edge badges. Unexplored tiles are never marked. The mark's sentence (`LensMark.Description`) is not read out by the WebGL board yet (the SVG board had it as a `<title>`).
- **Lens marks get the view** (`IMapLens.MarkFor(PlayerView, index)`, defaulting to `MarkFor(TileView)`), so a lens can read the rivers around a tile.
- **Yields** (`YieldLens`, G5): each explored or visible tile shows its total yield (`YieldCalculator.TileYields`: terrain, the best river on its edges, its improvement), tinted `rich` (4 or more), `poor` or `barren` (nothing, like desert), with a sentence naming each resource ("Plains: 2 food, 1 water a turn").
- **Movement cost** (`MovementCostLens`): each explored or visible tile shows its terrain's movement cost (tint `cheap` for 1, `costly` above), and ✕ (`blocked`) where land units can't go. River edges show their extra crossing cost (`+1`, `costly`) or ✕ (`blocked`) where land units can't cross; a free stream shows nothing.

## Rivers, borders and improvements on the board

- **Rivers:** `Helpers/SceneLayerBuilder` puts every known river edge in the scene (`SceneRiver`), its width per kind from `Helpers/RiverReliefs` (stream, river, deep river, so the kinds differ by more than colour) and its colour from the `--gv-river*` tokens, darker for the deep river and when explored. `RiverReliefsTests` checks every ruleset river has a width. The `/design` gallery shows each kind as a swatch (`.river-<id>` in `board.css`; `GameUiTests.BoardCss_StylesEveryRiver`).
- **Forecast:** the river bonus appears in the defender's strength breakdown ("+3 behind River").
- **Bridges** (G10a): each bridge in sight is a `SceneBridge` plank across its edge. The movement cost lens leaves bridged edges unmarked.
- **Territory borders** (G7): every edge of a visible territory tile whose neighbour is not in the same player's territory is a `SceneBorder`, dashed in the owner's seat colour and set slightly inside the tile so two players' borders on one edge stay apart.
- **Improvements and buildings:** each improvement in sight (the view carries it for visible tiles only) is a `SceneFeature` in the tile's upper left, so the unit or city in the middle stays readable. Buildings go in the same place (a tile holds an improvement or a building, never both): each building in sight (`TileView.Building`) with its roof in its owner's seat colour, and the building each of the player's cities is producing, see-through, on its reserved tile (`CityView.ProducingBuildingSite`). A finished building's log line points at its tile and pulses there (`BuildingCompletedEvent.Position`).

## Performance

Maps are 20×14 by default and at most 40×40 (`map.maxSize` in the ruleset). The board (F15 step 5, [plan](../plans/done/F15-step5-themes-touch-performance.md)) is checked on the bench at `/design/board` ([design gallery](#design-gallery-design)):
- **A new view** builds again only the layers whose part of the scene changed (`scene.js` compares each layer's input as text). A move that reveals nothing, the opponent's moves and the live echo of the player's own command rebuild at most the pieces; a move that changes any tile's visibility or contents rebuilds the whole terrain layer (and the features, which stand on the tiles), never the rivers or borders. On the full 40×40 bench, a march that reveals nothing fell from 110 to 220 ms (terrain every time) to about 6 ms, while the terrain layer alone takes about 70 ms; building the scene in C# (`BoardSceneBuilder`, about 30 ms on a desktop) is the other large part.
- **Frames** are drawn only after a change or while something animates. Labels are placed only when the camera moves or a label follows a moving piece, and only changed ones are written to the page; with the yields lens on the full map (about 1,700 labels) placing them takes about 4 ms of a panning frame.
- **What the GPU gets:** on the full map about 120,000 triangles of ground, rivers, buildings and borders and 530,000 of units (about 200 baked characters), drawn twice (the sun's shadow pass). A real opening is about 90,000 in all. Shadows, the pixel ratio (at most 2) and antialiasing are the same on every screen.
- **The bench numbers:** the scene built in C#, the last scene applied with each rebuilt layer's time, a full rebuild (a theme change), the time between frames while panning, labels and drawing within a frame, draw calls and triangles per layer, the pixel ratio and the shadow map size. A browser without a GPU (a cloud container renders in software) shows only before-and-after differences in frame times; the CPU-side numbers hold anywhere.

## Adding UI for a new mechanic

1. A new ruleset unit or terrain: add its `<symbol>` to `GameSprite` (and, for a terrain, its token in `tokens.css`, its `.terrain-<id>` icon rule in `board.css` and its relief in `Helpers/TerrainReliefs`). The tests name what is missing.
2. A new number or list on a panel: use `StatTable`, `ProgressBar`, `CostLabel`; add a planned component (`YieldList`, `QueueList`, `TreeView`) to `Components/Game` when its G item needs it, with tests and a gallery entry.
3. A new action: a button in `ActionBar` (unit) or in the fitting `CityPanel` tab (city), disabled with the rule's reason; a destructive one uses `ConfirmButton`. A new group of city content gets its own tab rather than lengthening another one.
4. A new selection kind: its panel in `SelectionCard`, reading `GameUiService`.
5. New map content: a part of `BoardScene` built in C# (`SceneLayerBuilder`) and a layer in `js/board3d` that only draws it. Information a player toggles on and off: a new `IMapLens` ([map lenses](#map-lenses)).
6. Update this document's catalog.
